\subsubsection{Definitions}\label{definitions}

Define the following terms/concepts

\begin{itemize}
\item
  a.) \textbf{Non-parametric statistics} {- A branch of statistics which
  does not assume that the sample data comes from a specific probability
  distribution. }
\item
  b.) \textbf{Parametric statistics} {- A branch of statistics which
  assumes the sample data comes from a specific probability distribution
  that depends on a set of fixed parameters.}
\item
  c.) Describe the advantages and disadvantages of
  \textbf{Non-parametric} significance tests

  Advantages

  \begin{enumerate}
  \def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
  \item
    {They may be the only alternative when sample sizes are very small
    (unless the population distribution is known exactly, but this is
    almost never the case)}
  \item
    {They make few assumptions about the population distribution of the
    data}
  \item
    {They are advantages when the data represent crude measurements such
    as subjective ratings/rankings (e.g, Likert responses)}
  \item
    {They often have simpler computations and interpretations than
    parametric tests}
  \end{enumerate}

  Disadvantages

  \begin{enumerate}
  \def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
  \tightlist
  \item
    {They are generally less powerful than their parametric analogues}
  \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item
  d.) \textbf{Explanatory Variable} {-In bivariate statistical
  procedures, the explanatory variable is the independent variable that
  usually defines the groups that will be compared across the response
  variable}
\item
  e.) \textbf{Response Variable} {-The dependent variable that measures
  the outcome of interest from each group}
\end{itemize}

\(\mu \in [-3.0, -2.4]\) \(\bar{x} = -2.7\)
\[ \text{Decision} = \color{red}{\text{reject} \ H_0} \\

The dermatologist is particularly interested in whether the new product
improves the appearance of the patients' acne. They plan to test the
null hypothesis \(H_0: p = 0.5\) using a right-tailed test with
alternative hypothesis \(H_A: p>0.5\) using a sign test to determine if
there is enough evidence to conclude that the topical cream has a
positive effect on reducing facial acne at the \(\alpha = 0.05\) level.

a.) Fill in the table above by computing the difference in acne scores
before and after applying the skin care cream. Record the sign of each
difference and report the total number of positive signs \(s\). Note
that any patient whose score did not change (a difference of zero) is
dropped from the test, so the number of observations used in the sign
test is the number of non-zero differences.

{Of the \(20\) patients, \(14\) had a positive difference, \(1\) had a
negative difference, and \(5\) had a difference of zero. The five tied
patients are discarded, leaving \(n = 15\) observations with \(s = 14\)
positive signs.}

b.) What does a ``positive'' sign indicate in this experiment? {A
positive sign indicates that the patient's acne score went down after
treatment - that is, the skin cream reduced the appearance of their
acne}

c.) Compute the \(p\)-value for the sign test {The number of positive
signs is \(s = 14\) out of \(n = 15\) non-zero differences. The
\(p\)-value is the probability of observing \(14\) or more positive
signs in \(15\) observations, given by
\[p\text{-value} = P(S\geq s | H_0) = \sum_{k = 14}^{15}\frac{15!}{k!(15 - k)!} \cdot 0.5^k \cdot (1-0.5)^{15-k} \]
\[ \approx 0.0005  \] }

evidence to conclude that the topical cream product has a positive
effect on reducing the appearance of acne. Interpret your decision in
context.

{Since the \(p\)-value \(0.0005\) is less than \(\alpha = 0.05\), we
reject the null hypothesis at the \(5\%\) significance level. There is
sufficient evidence to conclude that the topical cream reduces the
appearance of facial acne}

\(\bf (4.)\) The United States Supreme Court serves as the judicial
branch of the U.S. government, tasked with ensuring that laws conform to
the U.S. Constitution. Although traditionally operating as a relatively
discreet arbiter within the American government, recent years have seen
an unusual spotlight on the Supreme Court in partisan politics. This
heightened attention is largely attributed by political analysts to
several landmark Supreme Court decisions and ethical controversies that
have surfaced. Consequently, it is believed that this increased scrutiny
has adversely affected public approval of the Court across the political
spectrum. The table below provides a summary of current and historical
polling results for assessing SCOTUS job approval. The current approval
rating is summarized from \(192\) political polls conducted among the
public since late 2022 (Ryan and Bycoffe 2024). The historic approval
sentiment is characterized from \(39\) Gallup polls conducted since
early 2001.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\centering
\begin{tabular}{llrr}
\toprule
Period & Parameter & Sample Size & Mean SCOTUS Approval Rating\\
\midrule
\cellcolor{gray!10}{Current Approval (2022 - 2024)} & \cellcolor{gray!10}{$p_1$} & \cellcolor{gray!10}{192} & \cellcolor{gray!10}{41.16}\\
Past Approval (Gallup: 2001 - 2022) & $p_2$ & 39 & 50.87\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Conduct a two-sample test at the \(\alpha = 0.05\) significance level to
determine whether the current approval rating is significantly lower
than the historical average - be sure to address all five steps of the
hypothesis test.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  the pooled estimate of the common proportion is
\end{enumerate}

\[\hat{p} = \frac{0.4116(192)+0.5087(39)}{192+39} = 0.428\]

The success/failure condition must be checked in each group separately
using the pooled \(\hat{p}\): for group 1,
\(n_1\hat{p} = 192(0.428) = 82\) and
\(n_1(1-\hat{p}) = 192(0.572) = 110\); for group 2,
\(n_2\hat{p} = 39(0.428) = 17\) and \(n_2(1-\hat{p}) = 39(0.572) = 22\).
All four counts exceed the required \(15\), so the sample sizes are
sufficient to meet the asymptotic requirement of the test

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{1}
\item
  {The null hypothesis is \(H_0: p_1 - p_2 = 0\) and the alternative
  hypothesis is \(H_A: p_1 - p_2 < 0\)}
\item
  The pooled standard error is
\end{enumerate}

\[SE_{D_{pooled}} =  \sqrt{0.428(1-0.428)\left(\frac{1}{192}+\frac{1}{39}\right)} = 0.0869\]

the test statistic is

\[ Z_{obs} = \frac{(0.4116 - 0.5087)-0}{0.0869} = -1.11\]

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{3}
\tightlist
\item
  The \(p\)-value is given by
\end{enumerate}

\[p\text{-value} = P(Z \leq -1.11| H_0) = 0.132\] Note that your
\(p\)-value may be slightly different depending on how you rounded

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{4}
\tightlist
\item
  {At the \(\alpha = 0.05\) significance level, we fail to reject the
  null hypothesis of no difference and conclude that the current SCOTUS
  approval rating is not significantly lower than the historical average
  of about \(50.9\%\) approving }
\end{enumerate}

Health was interested in the number of reports of child abuse in
Sergipe, Brazil, before and after COVID-19 started. The researchers
hypothesized that children in abusive homes might be in more danger

hypothesis test. (Note that this proportion is the share of all
registries that involve a child under 12, not the underlying rate of
abuse in the population.)

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  the pooled estimate of the common proportion is
\end{enumerate}

\[\hat{p} = \frac{18+16}{70+53} = 0.276\]

\(\hat{p}\): for group 1, \(n_1\hat{p} = 70(0.276) = 19.3\) and
\(n_1(1-\hat{p}) = 70(0.724) = 50.7\); for group 2,
\(n_2\hat{p} = 53(0.276) = 14.6\) and
\(n_2(1-\hat{p}) = 53(0.724) = 38.4\). Note that \(n_2\hat{p} = 14.6\)
falls just below the guideline of \(15\), so the normal approximation
here is borderline and the results should be interpreted with some
caution

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{1}
\item
  {The null hypothesis is \(H_0: p_1 - p_2 = 0\) and the alternative
  hypothesis is \(H_A: p_1 - p_2 \neq 0\)}
\item
  The pooled standard error is
\end{enumerate}

\[SE_{D_{pooled}} =  \sqrt{0.276(1-0.276)\left(\frac{1}{70}+\frac{1}{53}\right)} = 0.0819\]

the test statistic is

\[ Z_{obs} = \frac{(0.26 - 0.30)-0}{0.0819} = -0.488\]

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{3}
\tightlist
\item
  The \(p\)-value is given by
\end{enumerate}

\[ p\text{-value} = P(|Z|\geq|-0.488||H_0)\]
\[ = 2\times \left[1 - P(Z < 0.488|H_0)\right] = 0.625\]

Note that your \(p\)-value may be slightly different depending on how
you rounded

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{4}
\tightlist
\item
  {At the \(\alpha = 0.01\) significance level we fail to reject the
  null hypothesis and conclude that the proportion of abuse registries
  involving children under 12 years of age before and after the start of
  the COVID-19 pandemic are not significantly different}
\end{enumerate}

significance level to determine whether fitness as a motivating factor
is significantly different between team and individual sports. Assume
the populations have the SAME variance.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  {The null hypothesis is \(H_0: \mu_1 - \mu_2 = 0\) where \(\mu_1\) is
  the mean score for fitness for team sports and \(\mu_2\) is the mean
  score for fitness for individual sports. The alternative hypothesis is
  \(H_A: \mu_1 - \mu_2 \neq 0\)}
\item
  The pooled estimate of the sample variance is
\end{enumerate}

\[s_{pooled} = \sqrt{\frac{(203 - 1)1.27^2 + (62-1)0.94^2}{203+62 - 2}} = 1.201 \]

the estimated standard error assuming the samples are independent and
have the same variance is given by

\[ SE(\hat{\mu}_d) = 1.201\cdot\sqrt{\frac{1}{203}+\frac{1}{62}} = 0.174\]

The test statistic is

\[ t_{obs} = \frac{(7.79 - 8.40) - 0}{0.174} = -3.505\]

where \(t_{obs}\) is approximately \(t\)-distributed with
\(203+62 - 2 = 263\) degrees of freedom

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\tightlist
\item
  The \(p\)-value is
\end{enumerate}

\[ p\text{-value} = P(|t|\geq |t_{obs}||H_0) \]
\[= 2\left[P(t \geq 3.505 | H_0)\right] = 0.00054\]

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{3}
\tightlist
\item
  {At the \(\alpha = 0.05\) significance level, we reject the null
  hypothesis and conclude that fitness as motivator is significantly
  different for athletes in team sports vs individual sports.}
\end{enumerate}
